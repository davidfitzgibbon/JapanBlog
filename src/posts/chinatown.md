### Chinatown

I very much needed to recover on Saturday, but on Sunday it was back out to Chinatown!

{% fig "/img/w8/china4.jpg" "Chinatown! Really felt like another country." %}

I'd never been to a Chinatown before. This was about x4 blocks of Yokohama, the biggest Chinatown in Japan apparently. And wow, it really felt like another country. The whole architecture and language seemed to change!

Honestly I was kinda overwhelmed. Maybe a little culture shock? After spending to long trying to get the hang of Japan over the years, this felt like being back to square one. I wasn't sure what any of the food was, or what might be in the shops. I guess it was a little breath of fresh air too.

{% fig "/img/w8/china2.jpg" "Our crew of humble adventurers!" %}

We found lunch in a Taiwanese vegetarian place that one of the crew knew about. It was pretty nice, though the Mapo Tofu didnt really live up to Eni's standards.

{% fig "/img/w8/china3.jpg" "Horsin' around" %}

There were lots of amazing sights to see, but weirdly we spent most time in a random museum of illusions that we found!

{% fig "/img/w8/china5.jpg" "Eni sees a rainbow, Eni gets a photo." %}

There were some pretty cool effects that I wont spoil here, and we got a discount to do an escape room after too. The escape room was set in a Chinese Restaurant 🤣.

All in all, it was a great afternoon, but I think I need to do a little more research to really get the most out of it again!

### Visiting friends

As if that wasnt enough, we also had Tuesday off. So for that we went out to visit a friend of Enis.

It's tradition in Japan that when you got to someones house, you bring them a little something. You wouldnt want to bring an item, because then they'd have to bring it out any time you came around. You'd be giving a burden, rather than a gift. So you give a little treat.

I found that cake / chocolate shop I mentioned earlier and got these little bad boys.

{% fig "/img/w8/visit1.jpg" "The snow work was amazingly delicate." %}

x5 chocolate covered cakes, made to look like famous Japanese mountains!

You could get whopper big ones that were the size of a regular cake. These were generous mouthfuls, and still about €20, which in Japan is CRAZY. They seemed to go down well though, even getting an oiishi (tasty!) and sugoi (cool!) from the kids of the house!

### What's around the corner?

This week on what's around the corner, it's the random train that's at the end of our road, about as far away from a train track as you can get in Machida!

{% youtube "ksS_Cz4As38" "What's around the corner?" %}

And when I mention the emergency road, it's this yoke.

{% fig "/img/w8/emergency.jpg" "This whale is too happy for the trouble he causes." %}

So essentially, it's a road with different traffic rules compared to most of the others in town. Most roads, pedestrians have right of way, all cars need to be ready to stop. If there's a traffic light, cars have right of way forwards, but not for turning.

Unless the mustachioed carp is hanging from the streetlights.

Any emergency services use these roads as highways to get to where they need to get to quickly. That's great, and I'm sure those in difficulty really appreciate it. Our issue is that, because it's at the end of our street, we get every emergency service in Machida blaring up the road!

It's not so bad, and we're kinda used to it already, but it was really confusing the first night or two!
