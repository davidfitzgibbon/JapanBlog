This week was mainly chilling. It's been a mad few months and we've basically been going non-stop. So we've been taking it very easy.

The above photo is from the window where I do my lesson plans!, that's Mt. Fuji peeping it's wee head above the clouds just in the middle. Delightful.

### Sushiro

We discovered Sushiro in Machida. It's a massive sushi chain over here, but the food is actually brilliant.

It's the same idea as the kaiten sushi we had before with the conveyor belt, but this place is a little snazzier. You order on an iPad kinda device, and then your order zips around on the conveyors, but pulls in to your table like it's a little train stop!

{% video "/img/w7/ro4.mp4" "An order coming in to our table!" %}

The menu was huge, and went from the classics to some more fusion ideas.

The tuna was maybe the worst part. A little dry and chewy. That's where the negatives end.

{% fig "/img/w7/ro3.jpg" "Pretty uninspiring" %}

I started with this salmon, mozzerella and basil pesto number, kinda expecting it to be not so great. A novely to try, and then pass over. But it was amazing! All the flavours so gentle, but matched perfectly. Not only did I start with this, but I finished with it too. The rice alone was delicious, tasting of basil on its own, implying the rice in this place is individually flavoured to match each dish!

Genuinely one of the nicest things I've ever eaten!

{% fig "/img/w7/ro1.jpg" "Salmon, mazzerella and basil pesto sushi. 10/10" %}

Next up was some simple chicken with a spicy sauce.

Spice in Japanese restaurants is one of two levels in Japan. Zero or a billion. This place was at zero. The chicken had TWO symbols for fire and chili on the menu. I don't think there was any spice at all. But that's ok, because holy heck it was tasty! I dont know how they can keep so much juicy fat inside through the cooking process, but also not have the fat leave a horrible texture. It was super tender, all the right flavours, and lovely crisp on the outside.

{% fig "/img/w7/ro2.jpg" "Glorious karaage (fried chicken.)" %}

Finally we had dessert. This was "sweet potato parfait, two ways". Japan loves sweet potato as a dessert ingredient, and they're dead right about it.

This Sundae had:

- Caramelized sweet potato chips
- Sweet potato sorbet
- Sweet potato parfait
- Sweet potato mousse
- Cream

Me oh my it was nice. I could take or leave the chips on top, but the rest together was great. Eni's not a fan of desserts that are too sweet and this passed her test too!

{% fig "/img/w7/ro5.jpg" "Sweet potato partfait (more than) x2 ways" %}

All in all we ate 15 dishes, the two desserts and four mugs of highballs (whiskey soda) between us.

That all came to about €25. Total, for the two of us. Sushiro we love you and will be back!

{% fig "/img/w7/ro6.jpg" "A mug of Suntory's finest" %}

### What's around the corner?!

It's been a while since we had a bit of "What's around the corner", so this time a double helping!

{% fig "/img/w7/shinjuku.jpg" "A castle on a skyscraper" %}

We were ambling around Shikjuku and noticed the castle on top of this building! Looks like the theme continues on the sides of the building too. Not sure what it is, but it sure stood out!

Then today I was walking to Yokohama and noticed this temple. I didn't think much of it at first.
{% fig "/img/w7/yokohama1.jpg" "Just a temple?" %}

But then I noticed it was raised on those huge supports, and that the path from the hill was linked to it, and over to the top of the next building!

{% fig "/img/w7/yokohama2.jpg" "Why not just raise the roof?" %}

I've tried to mark out where you can walk here. So to keep a bit of life in the building, they just raised it 50m into the air, and stuck it to the surrounding buildings and hillside. Awesome!
